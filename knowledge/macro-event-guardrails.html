1. The Mathematics of Implied Volatility Crush & Earnings Gaps

Binary macro releases (such as Federal Reserve FOMC rate decisions and Consumer Price Index inflation prints) and single-stock earnings announcements introduce severe discontinuity risk into equity portfolios. Prior to a binary catalyst, options markets price in expected movement via implied volatility (\(\sigma_{IV}\)). Following the print, IV undergoes an immediate volatility crush.

The expected percentage move (\(\sigma_{exp}\)) priced into an option straddle expiring closest to the event is modeled via implied volatility (\(\sigma_{IV}\)) and time to expiration (\(T\)):

$$\sigma_{exp} \approx \sigma_{IV} \times \sqrt{T} \times \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}$$

Carrying unhedged directional breakout risk across a binary window exposes traders to gap risk that frequently exceeds calculated technical stop-loss levels, bypassing order books entirely via pre-market or after-hours gapping.

3. Risk Management Protocols for Binary Catalysts

To insulate momentum portfolios from catastrophic earnings gaps and macro shocks, strict institutional guardrails must be enforced:

  • Mandatory Position Reduction: Reduce active swing-trading exposure by 50% to 100% prior to Tier-1 macroeconomic data releases or single-stock earnings reports.
  • Never Hold Unhedged Breakouts Through Earnings: Technical breakout setups lose their statistical edge when subjected to binary coin-flip outcomes; technical stop-losses fail to protect against overnight gap-downs.
  • Exploit Post-Catalyst IV Collapse: Rather than gambling on the binary outcome itself, institutional operators frequently wait for the volatility crush to clear, entering post-earnings continuation setups once structural directionality is re-established.
